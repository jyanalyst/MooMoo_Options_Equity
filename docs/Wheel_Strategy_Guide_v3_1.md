## STRATEGY OVERVIEW

The Wheel is a **conservative income strategy** that generates premium by selling cash-secured puts on quality stocks you genuinely want to own. If assigned, you sell covered calls to generate additional income and exit at a profit.

### Core Philosophy

| Principle | Why It Matters |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Quality Over Yield** | Only sell puts on businesses you'd hold 3+ years. Assignment is opportunity, not failure. |
| **Timing Over Frequency** | Wait for elevated volatility. Better premium from patience than constant deployment. |
| **Business Durability** | Prioritize recession-resistant sectors. Sustainable quality beats cyclical highs. |
| **Risk-Adjusted Returns** | Target 10-15% annual, not 30%+. Consistency over speculation. |

### The Edge

**Variance Risk Premium:** Implied volatility systematically exceeds realized volatility by 2-4% annually. You harvest this premium by selling insurance (puts) that typically expires worthless.

**Why it exists:** Behavioral bias — humans overpay for downside protection, especially during fear spikes.

**Your advantage:** Systematic execution during volatility spikes, on quality businesses with statistical edge.

## THE WHEEL CYCLE

### Phase 1: Selling Cash-Secured Puts

**Objective:** Collect premium while waiting to buy quality stocks at discount prices.

**Mechanics:**
1. Sell put option, receive premium upfront
2. Reserve cash to buy 100 shares if assigned
3. If stock stays above strike → option expires worthless, keep premium
4. If stock falls below strike → buy stock at strike price minus premium

| Outcome | What Happens | Next Step |
|---------|--------------|-----------|
| **Expires Worthless** (70-80% probability) | Stock stayed above strike | Keep premium, sell new put |
| **Assigned** (20-30% probability) | Stock fell below strike | Own shares at discount, move to Phase 2 |

**MooMoo platform note:** "Short Sell" on MooMoo options = Sell to Open. This is NOT margin short selling. Cash is reserved as collateral. You are selling an option you don't own, not borrowing shares.

**Example:**
- Sell KO $75 put, 42 DTE, collect $0.69 premium ($69)
- Stock closes at $79 at expiration → Keep $69, sell another put
- Stock closes at $72 at expiration → Own 100 shares at effective cost of $74.31 ($75 - $0.69)

---

### Phase 2: Selling Covered Calls (If Assigned)

**Objective:** Generate additional income while holding quality stock, exit when profitable.

**Mechanics:**
1. Own 100 shares from assignment
2. Sell call option above your cost basis
3. Collect premium regardless of outcome
4. If stock rallies to strike → shares called away at profit
5. If stock stays below strike → keep premium, sell another call

| Outcome | What Happens | Next Step |
|---------|--------------|-----------|
| **Called Away** (Stock rallied) | Sell shares at strike price | Realize profit + all premiums, restart Phase 1 |
| **Expires Worthless** (Stock flat/down) | Still own shares | Keep premium, sell new call or hold |

**Important:** You **want** stocks to be called away — it means the stock rallied and you profit. Holding indefinitely is not the goal.

**Covered-call entry parameters (mirror the put side):**
- Strike: **0.20–0.30 delta**, at or **above your cost basis** so assignment locks in a gain
- DTE 30–45; same IV Percentile floors, term-structure, and earnings rules as puts
- Exits: close at 50% profit, manage at 21 DTE, always close by 7 DTE
- **Ex-dividend watch:** an ITM short call risks early assignment around the ex-dividend date — check `get_dividends` before selling calls on dividend payers

## ENTRY PARAMETERS: THE GREEKS

### Delta: Probability of Assignment

**Target: 0.20-0.30 delta (70-80% probability of profit)**

| Delta | Assignment Risk | Use Case |
|-------|----------------|----------|
| 0.15-0.20 | 15-20% | Conservative, building position slowly |
| **0.20-0.30** | **20-30%** | **Sweet spot - optimal risk/reward** |
| 0.30-0.40 | 30-40% | Aggressive, higher premium but riskier |
| >0.40 | >40% | Avoid — too high assignment risk |

---

### IV Percentile: Timing Signal

**CRITICAL: the most important entry filter.** The scanner reports **IV Percentile** — the fraction of
the past year's daily implied-vol readings that sit *below* today's IV (measured against the persisted
IV history, **not** realized volatility). It appears as **"IVR"** in scan output and is referred to
colloquially as "IV Rank." A reading of 80 means current IV is richer than 80% of the last year.

| IV Percentile | Meaning | Action |
|---------------|---------|--------|
| **< 50** | Cheap premium | Do not deploy |
| 50–60 | Borderline | Normal/Aggressive regimes only |
| **60–80** | Rich | Ideal — standard deployment |
| **> 80** | Very rich | Size up (within the 20% cap) |

**Regime floors:** **> 60 in Cautious** (VIX 14–18), **> 50 in Normal/Aggressive**. Below the floor =
no trade, no exceptions. Provisional readings (< 20 IV-history observations) are unreliable — treat as
no-signal, not as a pass.

---

### VIX Regime: Market-Wide Timing

| VIX Level | Regime | Position Sizing | IV Rank Min | Max Positions | Max Deployment |
|-----------|--------|----------------|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| **<14** | **STOP** | **0%** | N/A | 0 | 0% |
| 14-18 | CAUTIOUS | 50% normal | **>60** | 2-3 | 50% |
| **18-25** | **NORMAL** | **100%** | **>50** | 4-5 | 70% |
| **>25** | **AGGRESSIVE** | **150%** | **>50** | 6 | 85% |

**Why VIX matters:**
- VIX <14: Premium sellers lose money risk-adjusted (academic research)
- VIX >20: Premium sellers make 2-3x normal returns
- Your edge exists in volatility spikes, not calm markets

**Set VIX alerts: 14, 18, 25, 30**

### Days to Expiration (DTE): Time Decay Sweet Spot

**Target: 30-45 DTE**

| DTE | Action |
|-----|--------|
| 7-14 | Avoid entry. Too little time, gamma risk high. |
| **30-45** | **Optimal. Accelerating theta, manageable gamma.** |
| 60-90 | Acceptable but capital tied up longer. |

**Exit rules:** Close at 50% profit OR 21 DTE (whichever first).

---

### Premium Target: Return on Capital

**Minimum: >1% of strike price per trade (>12% annualized)**

Example:
- Strike: $75 | Premium: $0.69 = 0.92% in 42 days
- Annualized: ~8% (borderline — acceptable at Cautious regime 0.5x sizing)
- At full size and Normal regime, target ≥1% per position

**Reality check:** If you're consistently collecting <1%, IV Rank threshold is too low or regime doesn't support deployment. Wait.

---

### Term Structure (Contango vs Backwardation)

**This is a mandatory filter — not optional.**

| Term Structure | Meaning | Action |
|----------------|---------|--------|
| **Contango** | Near-term IV < far-term IV | ✅ Favorable — proceed |
| **Flat** (<5pt spread) | IV similar across expirations | ⚠️ Cautious regime only if IV Rank >70 |
| **Backwardation** | Near-term IV > far-term IV | 🚫 STOP — Do not trade |

**Backwardation signals near-term stress.** Premium looks elevated but the market is pricing in an imminent event. Stand aside regardless of IV Rank attractiveness.

## POSITION SIZING FORMULA

```
Position Size = (Capital × 15%) × VIX Multiplier

Components:
- Capital: $50,000 (current)
- 15%: Base allocation per position
- VIX Multiplier: 0x (<14), 0.5x (14-18), 1.0x (18-25), 1.5x (>25)

Example ($50,000 capital, VIX = 17 Cautious):
  Base = $50,000 × 15% = $7,500
  VIX adjust = $7,500 × 0.5 = $3,750 adjusted deployment target
  BUT minimum contract unit = strike × 100
  Use smallest qualifying strike that fits within 20% hard cap ($10,000)

Example ($50,000 capital, VIX = 21 Normal):
  Base = $50,000 × 15% = $7,500
  VIX adjust = $7,500 × 1.0 = $7,500
  Find strike where strike × 100 ≈ $7,500 (i.e., ~$75 stocks)
```

**Hard cap: Never exceed $10,000 (20% of capital) per position regardless of regime.**

## EXIT RULES: DISCIPLINE OVER HOPE

### Mandatory Closes (Non-Negotiable)

| Condition | Action | Why |
|-----------|--------|-----|
| **50% Profit** | Close immediately | Managing winners at 50% improves win rate and cuts tail risk vs holding to expiry. Capital velocity. |
| **21 DTE** | Close if profitable | Gamma risk accelerates in final weeks |
| **2x Premium Loss** | Close immediately (stop loss) | If collected $0.69, close when option worth $1.38 |
| **7 DTE** | ALWAYS close (even at max loss) | Gamma risk too high to hold |

**Why not hold to expiry?** The last 50% of profit requires disproportionate time and gamma exposure. Close at 50%, free capital, redeploy. Capital velocity compounds returns.

### Risk Escalation

| Condition | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| 21 DTE AND down 50-100% | Roll to next month for credit OR close |
| 14 DTE AND down >100% | Close OR roll with credit — never for debit |
| 7 DTE | Always close |

**Rolling mechanics:**
- Buy back current position (debit)
- Sell next month contract (credit)
- Must result in net credit (even $0.01)
- Never roll if thesis is broken

### Black Swan Protocol (VIX >40)

1. STOP all new positions immediately
2. Close profitable positions to raise cash
3. Evaluate each underwater position:
   - Quality stock (KO, MNST, EW) → Hold or roll, will recover
   - Speculative → Close at loss
4. Wait 48-72 hours for volatility to stabilize
5. Reassess with clear head

## EARNINGS MANAGEMENT

**Absolute rule:** Reject any stock with earnings between entry date and expiration + 7 days.

**How to verify:**
1. Scanner output includes earnings status — check `earnings_status` column
2. Manual verification: Yahoo Finance company page → Key Stats → Latest Earnings Report + next estimate date
3. MooMoo: Company detail page shows earnings dates

**Post-earnings opportunity:** Selling puts AFTER earnings when IV crushes can be profitable, but only if:
- Stock didn't gap down significantly
- You still want to own at new price
- IV Rank still elevated (>40 post-earnings)

## TRADE EXECUTION: MOOMOO PLATFORM

### Order Entry (Cash-Secured Put)

1. Options Chain → Select expiration (30-45 DTE)
2. Filter: Put, OTM strikes
3. Find target strike (0.20-0.30 delta)
4. Side: **Sell** (labeled "Short Sell" in MooMoo = Sell to Open, NOT margin)
5. Order Type: **Limit**
6. Price: **Mid price** (bid + ask / 2)
7. TIF: **Day**
8. Contracts: calculated per position sizing
9. Confirm: Amount shows as "Credit" ✅

### Order Entry (Buy to Close)

1. Find position in Options tab
2. Side: **Buy** (labeled "Buy Back" in MooMoo)
3. Order Type: **Limit**
4. Price: Current bid or slightly above
5. TIF: Day

### Pre-Market Order Placement (SGT)

Limit orders placed before market open (before 22:30 SGT) will queue and attempt fill at open. Risk: if stock gaps significantly overnight, your limit may not fill. Safe practice: place limit orders in the SGT evening, verify fill at 22:30 open.

## CRITICAL RULES: THE 10 COMMANDMENTS

1. **Never sell puts on stocks you would not own** — If assignment terrifies you, don't trade it
2. **Never exceed 20% position size** — $10,000 hard cap at $50K capital
3. **Never ignore stop losses (2x premium)** — Hope is not a strategy
4. **Never trade through earnings** — Exit or roll 7 days before. No exceptions.
5. **Never deploy when VIX <14** — Premium insufficient, edge absent
6. **Never trade below the regime IV floor** — >50 IV Percentile (Normal/Aggressive), >60 (Cautious)
7. **Never use leverage — cash-secured only** — The 1.5x VIX figure scales position *size* within deployed cash, not borrowing. No margin, ever.
8. **Never skip journaling** — Can't improve what you don't measure
9. **Never revenge trade** — Take loss, walk away, come back tomorrow
10. **Never abandon system during drawdowns** — Drawdowns are normal. Worst time to quit is after losses.

## COMMON MISTAKES & HOW TO AVOID THEM

**Mistake 1: Chasing premium** — Selling 0.40+ delta for higher income. Fix: Never exceed 0.30 delta. If premium looks too good, the delta is too high.

**Mistake 2: Selling in calm markets** — VIX <14, low IV Percentile. Fix: Set VIX alert at 14. Never trade below the regime IV floor (>50 Normal, >60 Cautious). Patience is the edge.

**Mistake 3: Over-diversification** — Running 10+ positions. Fix: 4-6 concurrent maximum. Quality over quantity.

**Mistake 4: Holding winners past 50%** — "I'll wait for max profit." Fix: Set alerts at 50%, close immediately. Capital velocity beats premium maximization.

**Mistake 5: Assignment panic** — Immediately selling assigned shares at a loss. Fix: Only trade stocks you'd genuinely hold. Assignment is Phase 2, not failure.

**Mistake 6: Emotional thesis** — "I love this stock" or "it's near support." Fix: Business thesis must survive assignment. If you can't explain why you'd hold for 12+ months at strike price, don't trade it.

**Mistake 7: Ignoring term structure** — Trading into backwardation for elevated premium. Fix: Backwardation is a hard disqualifier. The market is pricing near-term stress. Stand aside.
